3. THE ONE REAL LIMITATION - NO SERIES / LESION KEY
================================================================================
  The filenames carry no patient, sequence or lesion identity. Consequences:

  a) Perceptual near-duplicates cannot be resolved.
     pHash Hamming sweep, within BTSC:

       threshold    pairs
           0          12
           2         161
           3         161
           4         716
           6        1957

     The step pattern (flat through 3, then climbing) matches the OpenMed set,
     so ~161 pairs at threshold <= 3 are genuine near-identical candidates.
     They are almost certainly adjacent slices of the same scan. With no group
     key there is no way to separate "adjacent slice" from "true duplicate",
     so they cannot be acted on.

  b) A leakage-safe split CANNOT be built for BTSC alone.
     There is no group to hold together. Splitting these 3,064 randomly will
     place adjacent slices of the same patient in both train and validation,
     producing an optimistic validation score.

  This limitation matters more than anything else in this report.

5. IMPLICATIONS FOR COMBINING
================================================================================
  1. DISJOINT - safe to merge. No duplicates are created by combining.
     Combined total: 10,056 + 3,064 = 13,120 pairs.

  2. CHANNEL MISMATCH - BTSC is 1-channel greyscale, OpenMed is 3-channel RGB.
     The serving pipeline stacks greyscale to 3 channels, so this is workable,
     but the training data loader must handle both.

  3. THE BLOCKER IS THE MISSING GROUP KEY.
     OpenMed has 348 lesion groups and a verified leakage-free split.
     BTSC has no group identity at all.

     If the two are merged and split by file, BTSC's adjacent slices will
     straddle the train/validation boundary and inflate the validation score.

  RECOMMENDATION (preferred):
     Assign all 3,064 BTSC slices to TRAIN ONLY. No BTSC slice ever appears in
     validation or test, which stay OpenMed-only where grouping works. This
     keeps the extra training volume without corrupting the evaluation.

  ALTERNATIVE (arguably more valuable for the SRS):
     Use BTSC as a held-out EXTERNAL test set. It is a different source, so it
     would yield a genuine generalisation number - which the OpenMed set alone
     cannot provide, since all of its 10,056 pairs come from one archive.
     This directly addresses limitation 1 in the OpenMed cleaning report.
